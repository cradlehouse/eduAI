One step at a time

Each screen has one job. The icons on the left are the film's steps in order, and a gold dot means that step still has something waiting. You build the pieces first (cast, locations, props). Then you pull those pieces into a scene, shoot it one shot at a time, and cut it. The asset drawer only opens where you're putting pieces together. The prompt bar only appears where you're making an image or a clip.

The example film is a diner at night. Angie tells Bert she's leaving town.

1 · Script

Job: get the story in, find out what needs building.
  1. Paste or write the script. Set the film's three settings once: shape (16:9), look, engine.
  2. Break it down. The AI lists who's in it, where it happens, what objects matter, and the scenes.
  3. Check the list (remove anything wrong), then one button: Next: make the cast.
Makes: empty Cast, Locations and Props pages, each with its description from the script.
Not here: any image making. Questions the script can't answer ("what does Angie wear?") move to Angie's page, where they're answered.

2 · Cast

Job: decide what each person looks like, once.
  1. Pick a person from the list (Angie). Her description is already filled in from the script. Edit it, and answer the gold questions.
  2. Generate three portraits. If you have a photo or drawing to start from, drop it in start from an image.
  3. On the one you like: This is Angie (that's her look now). Or Start from this, which puts it in the image slot so you can nudge it.
  4. Tab 2, Turnaround: front, side, back from the chosen look. Tab 3, Voice. Each is its own small step.
Makes: Angie, an element that can be dragged into any scene.
Not here: locations, cameras, dialogue, video. The prompt bar only makes portraits of this person.

3 · Locations

Job: build each place once, so it's the same place in every cut.
  1. Pick a location. Tab 1, Master wide: same as a character. Describe it, generate three, choose one. That wide is the location.
  2. Tab 2, Angles: the page suggests the angles scene 1 needs (counter, hatch, booth). Each one is made from the master wide, so it's the same room.
  3. Tab 3, Times of day if the script comes back here in daylight.
Makes: Diner inside: one wide plus its angles, draggable into scenes.
Not here: people. Locations are built empty, and the cast get put in them at the Shot step.
4 · Props works exactly like Cast, with the pie in place of Angie: describe it, generate, choose. It's optional; most short films have one or two props that matter, and the rest can be left in the scene description.

5 · Scene

Job: say where it is, who's in it, and what happens.
  1. The asset drawer opens on the left with everything you built. Drag the location into Where, and the people into Who. From the script they're usually there already.
  2. What happens is the scene from the script as action lines and dialogue. Change it here if the scene has changed.
  3. Plan the shots. The AI suggests a shot list (wide, over Angie's shoulder, close on Bert…), which you edit on the next screen.
Makes: the scene setup, which every shot in it inherits.
Not here: generating. No prompt bar on this screen, because nothing is being made yet.

6 · Shot

Job: get one shot right, then move to the next.
  1. Camera: pick the framing from pictures (wide, medium, close, over the shoulder) and the camera move. The page picks the location angle to match.
  2. Frame: the prompt bar already has Angie and the diner angle in it, and the description comes from the scene. Make frames (cheap stills), then Use this frame.
  3. Clip: the chosen frame plus her line becomes a video. Make 2–3 takes and choose one. Then the next shot in the list on the left.
Makes: a chosen take for this shot. It appears in Edit.
Not here: building people or places. If Angie looks wrong, fix her on her Cast page and every shot picks it up.

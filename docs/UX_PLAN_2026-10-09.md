## What the walkthroughs found that the first review missed

- **"Break it down" makes duplicates on a second run.** It doesn't match existing items under a different name:
  "EXT. DINER" vs DINER EXTERIOR, "RAG" vs "RAG / CLOTH". On 30 Sep this produced a second scene, BOB beside an
  orphan TRUCKER, and two rags. The first review's "Scene 2 has no location or cast" was the duplicate scene.
- **Re-running "Break it down" wiped a scene's location.** Unticking the duplicate "EXT. DINER" cleared scene 2's
  DINER EXTERIOR, despite "Existing entries are never overwritten".
- **It rains indoors in the finished film.** The INT. location description the breakdown wrote says
  "rain-streaked window", and that goes into every shot. Both example texts students see suggest rain ("Rain on
  the roof", "rain on the glass"). The breakdown also invented "raining" outside.
- **Descriptions carry action.** Prop: "slice of cherry pie passed through the kitchen hatch". A costume detail
  became a prop ("reading glasses on a chain").
- **Ages are ambiguous.** "50s" produced 1950s-styled young women. "Woman in her late 50s" worked.
- **Content.** BOB's looks came back bare-chested in an org marked "has minors". The prompt didn't ask for it.
  Nothing checks generated images.
- **Money.** $1,205 was allocated across productions against a $600-per-term plan, with no warning. One click
  ("Make 2 takes 1,080") can spend two-thirds of what's left.
- **Edit looks finished when it isn't.** It says "2 of 2 shots chosen" and offers Download while other scenes have
  no shots.
- **Deletes are instant.** "Delete entry" and "Delete project" (next to Save) have no check.
- **Bugs:**
  - "Making it…" stays after results arrive (seen live three times);
  - the account menu opens behind the page;
  - setting a location drops "NIGHT" from the heading;
  - "Read the brief" lands on a scene;
  - applying a breakdown can report success when saves were refused;
  - the Script undo log keeps the stale heading.
- **Phone:**
  - the Generate panel covers candidate pictures and their buttons;
  - the breadcrumb runs together;
  - no budget is shown;
  - empty scene slots sit below the asset list.
- **Viewers see a full editor** on productions they're not crewing. The database blocks writes correctly, so it's
  confusing rather than unsafe.
- **Admin:**
  - Niloo's invite expires 13 Oct, and invites aren't emailed;
  - demo invites are pending until 2027;
  - all six modules show as open;
  - people are called apprentice / student / enrolled on different pages;
  - a student account is named "admin".
- **Small:**
  - grey example text looks like real content;
  - the same action is called "Use this look" on props and "This is X" on cast;
  - sound tracks show 0:00;
  - "added to the bible" uses internal jargon;
  - the frame check runs on Claude Opus (`lib/script/framecheck.ts:64`), outside the Claude-only-where-needed rule.

**Why Bert was doubled (checked in the request sent to the image model).** The frame request describes Bert twice,
with different clothes:
- from the shot plan: "BERT stands at the grill **in a stained apron**…";
- from his Cast look: "BERT: Man in his 60s, grey stubble, **white apron and paper hat**".

**Tested, and that wasn't the main cause.** I rewrote the shot's "What the camera sees" to drop the clothes, which
is what a student can do. Bert was then described once, but the next two frames still had two Berts.

**The real cause is the reference picture.** Bert's Cast look is a front-on studio portrait: standing, facing the
camera, white apron, paper hat. In every frame the "extra" Bert on the right is that portrait, same pose and angle,
pasted into the room. The model also draws Bert at the grill, because the shot asks for it. It treats the reference
as a person to include, not as "this is what Bert looks like".

**The frames ignore the location reference too.** The kitchen master wide is an empty kitchen in daylight. The
frames show a different, night-time kitchen with hanging pans and steam, despite "keep the room exactly as in the
first reference". (The master wide being in daylight for a NIGHT scene is a separate problem.)

**A student can't fix this with prompts or settings.** Rewording didn't help, and they can't choose which reference
picture goes in. **To fix (not done; engine side):**
- use references for identity only, with a model or mode built for that, or build the frame in steps: the room
  first, then each person added into it;
- describe each person once;
- shot planning describes action, not clothes;
- run the frame check as soon as frames arrive, before the student picks;
- check the location look matches the scene's time of day.

**The app adds weather itself.** The same request contains "Indoors; the window glass is wet and dark outside",
though scene 3 says nothing about rain. Scene 1's rain is being carried across the whole film. **To fix (not done).**

Not walked: "start from an image" (upload), consent and releases, and downloading the exported file.

### The Edit screen: what's on it, and what's wrong with it

What each part is (checked in the live app):

- **Player:** plays the chosen take of each shot in order. "Shot 1 of 3: Angie close (scene 1)".
  "Not working? Open this shot" jumps to that shot.
- **Download the film / Export again:** export joins the chosen takes and the tracks below into one file. There's no
  sign of whether the export is current or still running.
- **Timeline rows**, each with a volume slider:
  - **Picture:** one block per chosen take, labelled "scene number. shot name": "1. Angie close", "1. Bert at hatch",
    "3. Wide". The leading number is the **scene**, not the shot. That's why two blocks say "1." and the next says
    "3.".
  - **ANGIE, BERT:** one row per speaking character, holding that character's voice for their lines (the dialogue is
    split from the clip and re-voiced in their Cast voice). A block sits under the shot where they speak.
  - **Room:** the location's room tone under each shot. The kitchen shot has nothing here because the kitchen has
    no room tone.
  - **Music:** one track under the whole film.

Why it's confusing:

1. **It doesn't say what's missing.** Scene 2 (Bob) and scene 4 (parking lot) have no chosen shots, so they don't
   appear at all. There's no gap or marker, so the film looks complete. "3 of 5 shots chosen" counts only planned
   shots.
2. **Marla isn't there**, although she's in the film. Her line is in shot 2, which has no take, and the Wide shot
   has no dialogue. Nothing explains the missing row.
3. **Numbers read as mistakes.** "1., 1., 3." are scene numbers. Students will read them as shot numbers.
4. **Characters are rows, not people in shots.** A row named BERT holds only his voice, not his picture.
   That isn't obvious.
5. **Empty room tone goes unflagged.** The kitchen shot plays with silence under it and no warning.
6. **The shot shown here is the doubled-Bert take**, chosen with "Use it anyway". Nothing on the Edit screen shows
   that a take was flagged.
7. **Nothing to edit.** No reordering, trimming or scene grouping; the sliders are the only controls.

What it could be:

- **Group the timeline by scene.** Every scene from the script appears, including unplanned ones, as a labelled gap
  with a link: "Scene 2 · EXT. DINER · not planned yet →".
- **Label shots by scene and shot** ("3.1 Wide") and show each block's length.
- **Label the voice rows as voices** ("ANGIE · voice"), and list the cast with no lines yet.
- **Flag missing pieces in place:** no room tone here, take flagged by the frame check, music shorter than the film.
- **Export shows its state:** "Export is up to date" or "Changes since last export", with progress while running.
- **Basic editing:** drag to reorder, trim the start and end of a take, a short fade between scenes.

**What this adds to the list:**
- **"Next" everywhere is a link to the exact screen and tab** that needs work, ordered by what blocks most:
  missing picture before missing room tone. This is the shared "what's next" check (item 1).
- **Paid buttons check the inputs they claim to use.** "Make frames" with no location master wide says so and links
  to it, with "Make frames anyway" as a choice. The same applies to clips for a speaking character with no voice.
- **Stock voices get free pre-made samples.** Paying is only for custom sample lines.
- **Loading feedback** on every navigation and button.
- **One name per thing:** "look" vs "master wide" vs "wide", "Crew" vs "Members".

## Decisions (Tim, 9 Oct)

- **Skipping "Plan the shots"** is allowed, but the student writes a reason. The educator sees it.
- **Production budgets:**
  - each production starts with **20,000 tokens**;
  - a **Budget step after Script** (option a) gives a rough estimate, revised after shot planning;
  - more money is requested with a reason and approved by the educator.
- **Content:**
  - generated images are checked;
  - flagged ones stay hidden until an educator signs off;
  - each educator or organisation sets its tolerance;
  - the checker should be **open-weight on our own servers** and reusable for other image checks.
- **Deletes:** every delete asks first and goes to a **Trash, restorable for 30 days**.
- **"Projects" become "productions".**
- **Last Call tidy-up and removal of "Advanced"** were approved and done 9 Oct.

## Where we differ from the OpenAI review

- **Rename:** "Make variations from this" misdescribes the button, which is free and fills the start slot.
  "Edit from this" fits.
- **Project posting:** fix the defaults (draft, starter budget) instead of a "Post anyway" dialog.
- **Scene screen:** clearer labels and a checklist, not a step-by-step wizard.
- **Role-by-role access tests:** more urgent than the review's ordering suggests.

## The list

### Stage 1 — honest, safe, tidy
1. **One shared "what's next" check** drives:
   - the step ticks;
   - the Script "Next:" line;
   - the student Home "Continue" card;
   - an honest Edit page ("1 of 3 scenes planned").
2. **"Plan the shots" readiness:** needs a location and the characters the script names in that scene. Skip with
   a reason, visible to the educator.
3. **"Break it down" merges, never duplicates or clears.**
   - It matches existing items by meaning, and asks when unsure.
   - It never clears what a student set.
   - Anything in no scene is flagged and doesn't block a step.
3b. **Script drafts, and asking before the script changes** (Tim, 9 Oct; replaces a fixed "reverse rule").
    - **When a change on another page would change the script**, a prompt offers:
      - **Add it to this draft** (default; tracked, with undo);
      - **Start a new draft** (the current script is saved as Draft N; the change goes into Draft N+1);
      - **Keep it on the card only** (for description edits; the script stays as it is).
    - **Drafts on the Script page:** numbered, with who and when. Each opens read-only with **Compare with now**
      and **Go back to this draft**. Going back saves the current script as a draft first.
    - **Saved automatically** before keeping a breakdown and before going back to a draft. **Note:** this protects
      the script's text only. "Break it down" changes Cast, Locations, Props and scene setups, not the script text,
      so the scene-location wipe has to be fixed in item 3 itself.
    - **Built 9 Oct:** Drafts panel (save with a note, compare with now, go back with confirmation), migration
      0132. Not yet built: the prompt on other pages ("add to this draft / new draft / card only").
    - **Decided (Tim, 9 Oct):** anyone on the crew can change the script and start a draft by default; who did it is
      recorded. Educators see drafts read-only, with compare.
3c. **Optional sign-off on script changes** (Tim, 9 Oct).
    - **Per-production setting, chosen by the educator:** script changes need sign-off from nobody (default), the
      writer, or the writer and the director.
    - **With it on, a change becomes a proposal.** It shows old against new (e.g. BERT's description before and
      after), who proposed it, and whether it goes into this draft or a new one.
    - **The people signing off are notified** with an in-app badge and a "Needs your sign-off" card on Home. Email
      comes later, once invites are emailed. They approve (the change goes in) or send it back with a note, and the
      student is told.
    - **If nobody holds the role**, the educator signs off, so work never gets stuck.
3d. **One "Needs your sign-off" queue for everything that needs approval:**
    - script changes (3c);
    - expensive takes (10c);
    - budget top-ups (10b);
    - daily-limit lifts (10d);
    - content-flagged pictures (item 7).

    Each person sees only what's theirs. Built once, straight after drafts, and reused by those items.
    **Principle (Tim, 9 Oct):** everything starts open to everyone on the crew. The educator, or a production
    itself, can make things more restrictive, never the other way round by default.
    **Built 9 Oct (script changes first):**
    - Crew page setting: anyone / the writer / the writer and the director. The crew can only make it stricter.
    - Script edits, lines added from scenes, undo and going back to a draft become requests.
    - The requester's own role signs straight away.
    - A change goes in only if the script hasn't changed since; otherwise it's marked failed and the proposer is
      asked to propose it again.
    - Queue on the Script page and on Home ("Needs your sign-off", "Waiting for others"), with see-the-change,
      sign off, send back with a note, and withdraw.
    - With sign-off on, only the educator can hand out signing roles.
    - Migrations 0133–0134; tested in the database as student and admin.

    **Notifications:** in the app for now. Email next, once sending is set up (needed for invites too): each request
    emails the signers with what changed and a link; approving happens in the app, not from the email. Text
    messages held back (phone numbers of under-18s, cost); maybe later for educators only.
    **Not yet:** spends, budgets, limits and content flags going through the same queue (built with Stage 2 and

4. **Breakdown writes clean descriptions:**

5b. **Rebuild how frame and clip requests are written.**

### Independent OpenAI retest — script as master and asset tracking (9 Oct)

The following was separately tested on a temporary production, **Advanced**, before it was
removed. It is useful counter-evidence for the intended late-add path, but does **not** disprove
the duplicate/overwrite failure found above: that failure happens when breakdown is run again
against existing, inconsistently named assets.

1. A new one-scene script was saved and broken down. The review screen proposed two characters,
   one location, two props, and one scene; each item was editable and tickable before commit.
   Its explicit promise was: "Unticked items are skipped. Existing entries are never overwritten."
2. Keeping that reviewed breakdown reported `5 added to the bible, 0 filled in` and set the
   project next step to building character looks and a location master wide. The extracted
   descriptions were materially clean in this simple case: place-only location, object-only
   props, and age/wardrobe character descriptions.
3. After the script was saved, a new character, **NORA**, was created from Cast and assigned to
   Scene 1. The add form asked exactly where it belonged in the script and, once a scene was
   selected, disclosed: "It's written into the script too. Undo it from the Script page."
4. The script subsequently contained one inserted NORA introduction. Its script page had an
   audit item: `NORA added to Scene 1`, with author, timestamp, and an Undo button. It did not
   duplicate the earlier extracted characters, location, or props.

**Conclusion:** The underlying one-way late-add mechanism is good and legible when it succeeds.
It should become the canonical mutation path. The work in item 3 (merge, never duplicate or
clear) is still essential because the same guarantees need to hold on re-breakdown, rename,
location addition, prop addition, and Undo—not only this simple character case.

**Additional acceptance tests required:**

- add a location and prop after the script, verify one script insertion and one asset each;
- re-run breakdown after every kind of late add, with aliases such as `RAG` / `RAG / CLOTH`;
- edit a Cast or Location description and verify the desired source-of-truth/sync rule;
- undo each mutation and confirm the script, scene membership, and asset list return to a
  consistent state with an explicit confirmation;
- test the same flows as student, instructor, admin, and view-only user.
6. **Deletes:** confirm every delete and add a 30-day Trash.
7. **Content sign-off:**
   - "fully clothed" by default in character prompts;
   - every generated image is checked;
   - flagged images are blurred until an educator approves;
   - per-organisation or per-cohort tolerance.
   The checker is chosen by the spike below.
8. **Bugs:**
   - stale "Making it…";
   - account menu behind the page;
   - heading loses "NIGHT";
   - "Read the brief";
   - breakdown reporting success when it failed;
   - undo log.

### Stage 2 — tokens and budgets
9. **Budget step after Script**, as a guided walkthrough.
   - **Rules of thumb:** about 3 shots per scene (fewer for a short scene); 5 takes per shot (3 rough on Fast at
     540 + 2 final on Quality at 720 = 3,060 per shot); looks at 120 each with one redo; sound and music.
   - **The walkthrough explains:**
     - why the budget looks the way it does;
     - what each change costs: one more shot ≈ 3,060, one more scene ≈ 9,300, one more character ≈ 120;
     - that the total is editable;
     - "Send to your educator".
   - Last Call works out at about 13,300.
10. **Budgeted vs spent:**
    - each budget line shows budgeted vs spent;
    - top-up requests with a reason;
    - educator approve, trim or return;
    - warn when productions are promised more than the plan has.
10b. **Admins can't actually top anything up today (checked 9 Oct).**
     - "Add tokens to a project" on the Overview links to the Cohorts page, which has no budget controls.
     - The only control is cohort → Projects → Edit → "Budget (tokens)". It sets a new total, not an amount
       added, and records no reason or who changed it.
     - Personal budgets exist in the database (`personal_budgets`, 0009) and are used as a fallback, but nothing
       anywhere lets anyone set one.

     **Proposed:**
     - **Admin Budgets page:** plan → cohort pot → productions, each showing allocated, spent and left. "Top up"
       takes an amount and a reason and is logged. It warns when promises exceed the plan.
     - **Educator requests queue:** approve, trim or decline, paid from the cohort pot up to a limit the admin sets.
     - **Student "Ask for more"** on the production and in the "Not enough tokens" message.
     - **Production history:** every top-up, who approved it and why.

     **Open question for Tim:** do students need personal allowances (e.g. for solo practice), or do only
     productions hold tokens?
10c. **Who can spend inside a production.** Today any crew member can spend everything; roles are credits, not
     permissions. Options considered:
     - **A.** Anyone on the crew (today).
     - **B.** The producer holds the money and approves every paid action.
     - **C.** Departments: budget lines from the Budget step tied to roles.
     - **D.** Equal personal shares within the production.
     - **E.** A cost threshold: cheap actions are free to the crew; takes need the producer's or director's OK.

     **Going with C + E** (Claude's recommendation; assumed 9 Oct unless Tim changes it).
     - A one-person crew holds every role.
     - The educator can always approve or override.
     - Every spend records who pressed it.
10e. **Delegated budgets, and sign-off for token and role changes** (Tim, 9 Oct).
     - **The educator can make a crew role the budget holder** (e.g. the producer or the director) for an amount,
       e.g. 250,000 tokens. The holder hands it out: to crew members, or to the budget lines from the Budget step
       (C in 10c). The holder can't hand out more than they were given.
     - **The chain:** educator → budget holder → crew. Every hand-out is logged with who, how much and why.
     - **Changes to tokens and to roles go through the sign-off queue (3d):** asking for more money (signed by the
       budget holder, or by the educator above their amount); changing who holds a role (signed by the educator
       when sign-off is on, already enforced for signing roles).
     - Daily and weekly limits (10d) still apply on top.
10d. **Daily and weekly limits as well as the total** (Tim, 9 Oct), so a production can't spend a month's money in
     a day.
     - Each production has a total (its budget), a weekly limit and a daily limit. Defaults would be something like
       a quarter of the budget per week and a tenth per day; the admin can change them.
     - The same idea applies per person within a production, so one crew member can't take the whole day's share.
     - **Friction:**
       - a heads-up at 75% of the day's limit ("You've used most of today's tokens");
       - at the limit, paid buttons stop with "Daily limit reached. Resets at midnight, or ask your educator to
         lift it";
       - the educator can lift it once, with a reason that's logged.
     - Students see all three, like Claude's usage meters: today, this week, total.
11. **Every generate button names the unit and what's left.**
    - It reads like "Generate 3 looks · 60 tokens · 1,590 left".
    - Adding a scene or shot shows its cost.
    - Warn before using more than half of what's left.

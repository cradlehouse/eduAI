# Stock voices: where they come from, and the path to licensed (2 Oct 2026)

The ready-made voices a student picks on Cast → Voice. Real people's voices (students, teachers) are a
separate route: only with a signed release, a guardian signing under 18 (`PRODUCTION_FLOW.md`).

## What's true today

| Part | Model | Trained on | Honest status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stock voice samples | **Kokoro-82M** (open weights, Apache) | Public-domain and Apache/MIT audio, under 12 hours of CC BY speech (Koniwa, SIWIS), **plus synthetic speech generated by closed commercial voice models** | Mostly openly licensed; the synthetic part isn't licensed and its sources aren't public |
| Turning each performed line into the character's voice | **Chatterbox** (Resemble, open weights, MIT) | "500k hours of cleaned data", sources not named. Every output carries Resemble's inaudible Perth watermark | Undisclosed |

What we can say, as fact:
- The stock voices come from an open model trained mostly on public-domain and openly licensed
  recordings, plus some speech generated by commercial voice models.
- We can't say the voices are licensed, consented or "ethically trained".
- Students never see a model or vendor name (`DESIGN.md`); this is for the mission page, the credits page
  and anyone who asks. The public wording is on the mission page.

Attribution: Kokoro's model card asks nothing extra for outputs. The CC BY datasets (Koniwa, SIWIS) belong
on the credits page.

## The path to licensed voices

| Step | What | Unlocks | Cost / when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Credits page** | Name Kokoro and its datasets; say plainly what's synthetic | Honesty now | Hours |
| **2. Paid, consenting actors** | Commission 8–12 stock voices from Texas voice actors under an AI rider: separate written consent for synthesis, a session fee plus per-use or annual residuals, the right to withdraw a voice for new work | "Stock voices are performed by paid actors who agreed to this use" | ~$10–25k incl. studio time (SAG-AFTRA AI sessions reported ~$957 per 4 h, *unverified*; non-union Dallas rates likely lower) |
| **3. A clean voice model** | Train a Kokoro/StyleTTS2-style model **from scratch** on LibriTTS-R (585 h, public-domain LibriVox, CC BY) plus the actors' recordings. Swap the parts trained on web data (PL-BERT on Wikipedia, WavLM with podcasts) | "Stock voices come from a model trained only on public-domain speech and paid, consenting actors" | ~$1–3k compute; ~6 months |
| **4. Clean voice conversion** | Replace Chatterbox: a HuBERT encoder trained on LibriSpeech plus our own vocoder on LibriTTS-R; or a Fairly Trained vendor if one appears for dialogue | The whole voice chain licensed or consented | 12–24 months; lower quality than Chatterbox expected at first |

**Why from scratch:** fine-tuning Kokoro or Chatterbox on licensed recordings doesn't remove what the base
model already learned. Only a model trained from the start on clean data supports the claim.

**Honest caveats that stay:**
- LibriVox readers put their recordings in the public domain but never specifically agreed to voice
  synthesis.
- Common Voice is CC0 but phone-quality: fine for transcription, poor for voices.
- ElevenLabs' voice library is consented and paid, but its base model's training data is undisclosed.

**Options considered and set aside:**
- Fairly Trained voice vendors (Voice-Swap, Kits, Vocalist, Voicemod) are singing-oriented.
- Ethovox (SAG-AFTRA) hasn't launched.
- Emilia-trained models use non-commercial or CC-tagged YouTube data.

Decision for Tim: budget and timing for step 2. The first move is quotes from Dallas voice talent.

# Brand identity brief (draft, 2 Oct 2026)

For a brand identity designer or studio. It says what the problem is and what we need, not what the
answer looks like. Read with `BRAND.md` (principles) and `research/brand-claims-2026-10.md` (what we
can and can't honestly claim today).

## The company in a paragraph

Imaje (working name) is a film school in the browser for schools and youth media programmes. Students
write a script, build their characters and locations, plan the shots, choose the takes and edit the film.
AI renders; students direct. Our first partner is Pegasus Media Project in Dallas, a nonprofit that runs a
free nine-month filmmaking apprenticeship and the largest youth-run film festival in the US. Most of its
apprentices are under 18, and its outreach is to women, BIPOC and LGBTQ+ young people.

## The problem

Our two audiences both distrust AI.

- **Young creatives** think it takes from artists, wastes energy and water, makes generic work and
  feels like cheating.
- **Schools and programmes** fear deepfakes of students, content they can't control, runaway costs and
  reputational damage.

We're selling two things in response: **protected** (shelter from the wildness of open AI tools) and
**principled** (building with the best tools without taking from other artists or from the planet).

Today, the existing language around this sounds earnest and corporate. We need an identity that makes
these values feel like something a 16-year-old filmmaker would be proud to be part of, and that a
programme director would trust in front of a board.

## Who it has to win

| Audience | What they need to feel |
|---|---|
| Students, 13–19, often AI-sceptical | This is a real filmmaking tool, it's mine, and using it isn't selling out |
| Programme leads and instructors | Safe, serious, under control |
| Parents and guardians | My child is protected |
| Funders (arts foundations, education grants) | Responsible, credible, worth backing |

## The hard part

Hold these together (more in `BRAND.md`, "Tensions to hold"):
- Safe, but not childish or institutional.
- Principled, but not preachy, and not anti-AI.
- Serious about craft, but welcoming on day one.
- Honest about what isn't solved yet, but confident.

## What exists already

- **Product visual system** (`DESIGN.md`): dark film-set canvas, warm white text, one gold accent for the
  primary action, cyan for "the chosen take", coral for problems; neutral sans type; "the images are the
  personality". The identity can extend or challenge this, but the product's colour meanings stay.
- **Principles** (`BRAND.md`): eight, with what each rules out.
- **A working name** that hasn't been tested. Naming is in scope if the designer thinks it should be.

## What we need

1. **Strategy check.** Push back on the principles and the two promises. Are they right? Are they
   distinctive?
2. **Verbal identity.** Name recommendation (keep, adjust or replace), positioning line, tone of voice,
   and the core messages for each audience. These must stay inside what we can truthfully claim today.
3. **Visual identity.** Wordmark and mark, colour (in harmony with the product), type, imagery and
   art-direction rules, motion principles.
4. **Applications.** Website home page, pitch deck template, one-page grant and funder sheet, app icon,
   end card and credit for student films (optional and off by default for festival entries), social
   templates.

## Constraints

- Imagery of real students only with a signed release that covers marketing (a guardian's under 18).
- No generic AI visual clichés: glowing brains, robots with cameras, purple-blue gradients, sparkles.
- No claims beyond what `research/brand-claims-2026-10.md` says is true today.
- Pegasus Media Project's own brand comes first in anything co-branded; Niloo signs off.
- Accessibility: AA contrast at minimum; colour never the only signal.

## Who we're looking for

A designer or small studio with identity work in **film, arts education or youth culture**, ideally one
that has worked with audiences sceptical of tech. Bonus: someone who has worked in Dallas or with
nonprofits and funders.
